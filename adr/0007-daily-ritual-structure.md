# 0007: Daily ritual structure and decision timing

**Date**: 2026-05-31
**Status**: Accepted

## Context

WFH removes the natural commute-as-transition, so daily context shifts must be deliberately
designed. The daily system also needs explicit decisions about *when* planning decisions
happen — ADHD decision fatigue means decision quality varies significantly through the day,
and making planning decisions at the wrong energy level produces poor choices, avoidance,
or over-ambitious lists that fail immediately.

Both problems share the same root: without deliberate structure, cognitive load bleeds
across contexts and decisions happen at whatever energy happens to be available.

## Decision

The day has three distinct cognitive moments, each with a specific job:

**Work signoff (~5pm) — decide and close work context:**
Sweep work channels, mark done, preview tomorrow's calendar, set tomorrow's Highlight,
migrate or drop incomplete items. Followed by a context break — something that makes
re-entering work require active effort rather than passive drift. This is the right moment
for next-day decisions: energy and context are still good.

**Morning planning (~8am) — confirm, don't deliberate:**
Both priority streams are already decided. Work = look up the sprint board (externally set).
Personal = confirm the Highlight pre-decided yesterday. Add effort estimates, sanity-check
fit against today's calendar. No fresh choices — morning confirms; it does not deliberate.

**Evening sweep (~9pm) — capture only, close the day:**
Review journal, brain dump to inbox, confirm tomorrow's Highlight if not already set.
No planning, no decisions. Decision quality at end of day is poor; the sweep closes loops,
it does not open new ones. Earns the day's completion signal.

These are two shutdowns, not one, because work and personal life need different closure:
the work signoff closes the professional context; the evening sweep closes the whole-day
cognitive loop. They are sequential, not redundant.

## Alternatives considered

**Single shutdown at 9pm** — rejected. Combining work closure with day closure at low
energy means making work-related next-day decisions at the wrong moment.

**Single shutdown at 5pm** — rejected. Work context closes, but the whole-day cognitive
loop (personal capture, journal) stays open, keeping the "not enough" loop running through
the evening.

**No formal shutdown** — rejected. Without explicit closure, cognitive loops stay open
("did I miss something?"), which prevents genuine rest.

**Decide everything in the morning** — rejected. Morning energy is good but work priorities
aren't fully known yet. Also leaves evenings without a shape for tomorrow.

**Decide everything at 9pm** — rejected. Decision quality is lowest at end of day.

## Consequences

- "Morning planning" is a misleading name if it implies deliberation — it is confirmation.
  The real planning moment is the work signoff the previous evening.
- The Highlight is pre-decided at work signoff and confirmed (not re-decided) at morning
  planning.
- The evening sweep is explicitly not a planning moment. Items that surface decisions
  during the sweep get captured and deferred, not resolved on the spot.
- The context break after work signoff is the WFH-specific commute replacement. The form
  is flexible; the principle is: relocate rather than rely on willpower.
- This structure depends on the work signoff actually happening. If it's skipped, morning
  becomes ad-hoc deliberation — the failure mode this design prevents.
